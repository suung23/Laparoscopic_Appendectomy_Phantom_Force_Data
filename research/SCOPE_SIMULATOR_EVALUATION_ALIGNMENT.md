# SCOPE v1 simulator evaluation alignment

Reviewed the local SCOPE v1 manuscript on 2026-10-06. The manuscript describes a bounded, phantom-grounded simulator for grasping, elevation, and release. It requires pivot geometry, instrument contact, deformable tissue with attachment constraints, and sensor observations. Its primary simulator test is transfer to held-out physical phantom conditions; performance inside the simulator is not the endpoint. Cutting, bleeding, and whole-procedure simulation are outside that manuscript's initial scope.

## Current fit

The appendectomy repository is a useful implementation and stress-test base, but it is not yet evidence for the manuscript's simulator evaluation. Existing bimanual/RL results and GPU contact comparisons are synthetic engineering checks. They do not calibrate material or contact parameters against physical phantom measurements, validate predictions on reserved physical trajectories, or show improved policy performance on held-out physical conditions. The repository currently contains no measured phantom trajectory set.

**Decision:** use the simulator now for software development and controlled synthetic ablations only. Do not report its internal reward, simulated force, or current RL success as phantom-validated performance or as evidence that simulation improves physical transfer.

The rollout evaluator now applies a numerical-integrity gate whenever a backend reports FEM health. An episode with non-finite state, non-positive signed element volume, inverted elements, or non-finite elements is stopped by default and cannot count as task success; `--continue-invalid-physics` is diagnostic-only and does not restore eligibility. Backends without these channels report health as unavailable, not passed. The 2026-10-06 RTX 4090 Newton smoke test failed this gate after 1.067 simulated seconds with 16 inverted tetrahedra (minimum signed volume ratio -0.766). See `validation/newton-physics-failfast-2026-10-06.json`. This guard prevents corrupted trajectories from inflating results; it is not evidence that tissue mechanics are accurate, and the current Newton appendectomy scene remains ineligible for manuscript evaluation.

A follow-up RTX 4090 run exercised the damped, force-capped grasp-assist path with the Appendix300 texture candidate enabled. It reached tool-tissue contact (peak tip-force channel 0.516 N; peak grip-force channel 75 mN; these channels are not physically calibrated) but stopped after 6.13 simulated seconds with two inverted tetrahedra (minimum signed volume ratio -0.606), before any grasp stage. The endoscope render confirms only a visual transfer candidate: the SAM2 prompt-mask ROI is not expert-verified and has no video-to-3D registration. Neither result qualifies as a manuscript evaluation; see `validation/newton-appendix300-compliant-grasp-runtime-2026-10-06.json`.

Per-organ telemetry localizes those inversions to the unpinned cecum, not the appendix. The worst cell is cecum-local tet 7280; its rest volume is at the 24th percentile, and its rest centroid falls within jaw/tool reach at the failure pose. A geometry audit finds this tet's circumradius/minimum-edge ratio at the 68th percentile, so its volume rank alone does not show a uniquely poor-shape outlier. A failure-only contact trace records all four nodes of this element in same-tick `left/jaw_left` soft-contact records; that link also contacts 354 cecum and 199 appendix particles in that tick. A matched-action three-seed ablation then changed only tool/tissue collision: all three contact-enabled paths inverted cecum, while all three collision-disabled paths stayed healthy with identical action and arm-joint trajectories. This establishes contact-triggered instability on these scripted trajectories, but does not validate or calibrate the contact law. Twenty VBD iterations still produced inverted cells. Doubling the physics rate produced more. Reducing the routed tool/shelf contact stiffness from 100 kN/m to 1 kN/m also did not prevent inversion. A 3/6/12 mm/s scripted approach sweep delayed contact and changed failure severity, but all three tested rates still inverted cecum and the response was non-monotonic. The `kf=0` run was a rejected no-op: source inspection of Newton VBD shows FEM-rigid contacts consume averaged `mu`, `ke`, and `kd`, not `ShapeConfig.kf`; all 70 saved arrays were therefore identical because active `mu` was unchanged. After wiring a true `contact_mu` override to both sides of VBD soft-rigid contact, a three-seed `mu=0` probe avoided inversion in all three episodes, but peak nodal speed rose to 85–136 mm/s, the uncalibrated wrench channel fell sharply, and all episodes completed zero stages. This is diagnostic sensitivity, not a usable material setting. A 10x reduction in rigid tool/shelf contact `ke` also left two cecum elements inverted and slightly worsened the minimum signed-volume ratio; its action path changed, so it is a sensitivity probe rather than a paired-action causal test. No setting is promoted: none is physically calibrated, and these episodes completed zero task stages. The repeated-run study also exposed stale contact manifolds on reset; reset now zeroes restored rigid-body velocities and recomputes contacts before emitting the initial observation, eliminating spurious initial wrenches of up to 299 kN. See `validation/newton-cecum-contact-inversion-diagnostics-2026-10-06.json`, `validation/newton-tool-tissue-collision-ablation-2026-10-06.json`, `validation/newton-demo-approach-speed-probe-2026-10-06.json`, `validation/newton-tool-friction-ablation-2026-10-06.json`, `validation/newton-tool-contact-stiffness-probe-2026-10-06.json`, `validation/newton-tool-mu-ablation-2026-10-06.json`, and the per-run `newton-backend-config.json` files under ignored local outputs.

An initial, dependency-free held-out scoring utility is available at `ct_appendectomy/evaluate_phantom_holdout.py`. It enforces specimen/build-disjoint development and held-out groups and reports XYZ force-vector error, displacement RMSE, relaxation force error, contact-transition timing/count/direction agreement, and specimen/build-clustered bootstrap intervals. Intervals are `null` with fewer than two held-out groups. Predicted signals must already be sampled at measured timestamps; no fitted time shift or force scale is allowed. Its synthetic self-test checks the metric implementation, split-leakage rejection, and interval path only; no phantom data have been scored.

The CSV contract is `split,specimen_or_build_id,trajectory_id,time_s,phase,force_measured_x_N,force_measured_y_N,force_measured_z_N,force_predicted_x_N,force_predicted_y_N,force_predicted_z_N,displacement_measured_mm,displacement_predicted_mm,contact_measured,contact_predicted`. Use `development` and `heldout` values for `split`; phases should distinguish `loading`, `unloading`, and `relaxation`. After the simulator is frozen, score a local synchronized dataset with `python ct_appendectomy/evaluate_phantom_holdout.py <local-csv>`. Do not commit raw phantom or animal data.

## Public data availability check

The Open-H Embodiment surgical phantom card describes synchronized video, robot state, and actions but explicitly lists no force/torque sensor; it may support an observation/action interface check, not mechanical calibration ([dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/PhysicalAI-Robotics-Open-H-Embodiment/blob/ff0dbf674812a6c84ff3cd30675529bd99b7ddef/Surgical/ucsd/surgical_learning_dataset/readme.md)). A published Zenodo force-estimation record contains stereo images, PSM states, and force/torque ROS bags, but its page does not specify a reuse license or document phantom deformation/relaxation ground truth; it remains a candidate for later rights and channel review, not a calibration source ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/21041361)). Neither dataset was downloaded or added to the repository.

A published silicone-phantom study describes synchronized actuator motion, ATI Nano17 force, marker displacement video, and ten repeat trials. Its manipulation is a simple vertical push/release, and its reported model check compares against FEA rather than reserved measured trajectories. It is a useful measurement-protocol precedent, not reusable ground truth for laparoscopic grasp/retraction ([study PDF](https://bartlab.org/newweb/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/2.pdf)).

## Evaluation path that matches the manuscript

1. Add a bounded grasp–elevate–release benchmark that can run without the full appendectomy scene while retaining the same tool/contact and tissue mechanics under test.
2. Record physical development and reserved test trajectories with synchronized tool pose, tissue/phantom displacement, measured load, contact state, timestamps, specimen/build identity, sensor calibration, and uncertainty. Split by specimen or build, not adjacent frames. Keep raw personal or identifiable material outside Git.
3. Fit parameters only on development trajectories, including loading, unloading, and relaxation. Preserve parameter ranges when several models fit; do not label fitted parameters as biological constants.
4. On reserved physical trajectories, report load–displacement error, relaxation response, contact-transition timing, numerical stability, controller timing, and pairwise action-outcome ranking where the physical protocol supports that comparison. Define acceptance bounds from instrument repeatability and specimen variability before scoring the held-out set.
5. Compare real-demonstration-only, uncalibrated-simulation augmentation, and calibrated-simulation augmentation with matched real-data and optimization budgets. Freeze each policy for evaluation on the same held-out physical phantom conditions. Report simulation/RL fine-tuning as separate conditions; the decision endpoint is physical transfer, including failures and added data/compute.

## Evidence gates

The manuscript may describe the simulator as calibrated only after development-set fitting is complete and a frozen parameter set is evaluated on reserved physical data. It may claim useful simulation augmentation only if the predeclared held-out physical endpoint improves over the matched real-only condition with uncertainty reported. If that test is unavailable or does not improve, report that outcome and retain the real-data route as primary. Visual similarity, CPU/GPU agreement, synthetic stability, and in-simulator task success do not substitute for either gate.

This note summarizes the manuscript's evaluation intent; the manuscript itself and its figures were not copied into the simulator repository.
